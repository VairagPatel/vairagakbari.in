## Application Checklist

Use this for EVERY application:

### Pre-Application (15-20 minutes)
- [ ] Read full job description carefully
- [ ] Identify 3 main business problems they're trying to solve
- [ ] Map your experience to each problem (specific examples)
- [ ] Research company (product, recent news, growth stage)
- [ ] Find decision-maker on LinkedIn (hiring manager if possible)
- [ ] Check for mutual connections

### Application Materials (20-30 minutes)
- [ ] Customize résumé summary for this specific role
- [ ] Select 2-3 most relevant portfolio links
- [ ] Draft tailored cover letter/message (if required)
- [ ] Proofread everything (check company name, role title)
- [ ] Verify all links work

### Submit (5 minutes)
- [ ] Apply through preferred channel (company site > LinkedIn > other)
- [ ] Save job description (they often get deleted)
- [ ] Log application in tracker (date, company, role, contact)
- [ ] Connect with hiring manager on LinkedIn (personalized note)

### Follow-Up (ongoing)
- [ ] Set reminder for 5-7 day follow-up
- [ ] Track response/no response
- [ ] Note any feedback received

```
I'm a growth operator with experience managing sales pipelines and fixing workflow bottlenecks.

Currently at Jumbo Homes, I manage property sales end-to-end—buyer qualification, visit coordination, negotiation, legal handoff. I also build automation where it makes sense, like a Gmail draft generator that freed up our supply team's time.

I'm drawn to founder's office roles because I like owning problems end-to-end, from diagnosis to implementation. That's what I'm looking for next—a role where I can take a business problem and see it through.
```

**"Why are you interested in founder's office roles?"**
```
I've realized I'm most effective when I can own a problem completely—from identifying the issue to implementing and measuring the fix.

At Jumbo Homes, when I saw our outreach process was inefficient, I didn't just flag it—I built the automation to solve it. When I noticed pipeline bottlenecks, I coordinated across teams to clear them.

Founder's office roles give me that end-to-end ownership. I can work on what matters most to the business right now, whether that's growth, ops, customer insights, or something else entirely.

I'm less interested in optimizing one metric within a narrow function and more interested in solving whatever's blocking the company's progress.
```

**"Walk me through a time you improved a process"**

Use **Seller Outreach Agent** example:
```
**Situation:** Supply team at Jumbo Homes was spending hours every week manually drafting personalized outreach emails to property sellers. Time that could've been spent on actual conversations.

**Task:** Find a way to automate the prep work without losing the personal touch.

**Action:**
- I mapped out the email structure and identified what could be templatized vs. what needed human judgment
- Built a Google Sheets workflow that pulled lead data and generated personalized Gmail drafts
- Key: Drafts went to human review—team could edit, approve, or discard. Automation handled prep; humans handled judgment.

**Result:** Supply team went from 2-3 hours/week on email prep to 15-20 minutes. More time for follow-up calls and relationship building.

**Learning:** Best automation keeps humans in the loop at decision points. Automate prep, not judgment.
```

**"What's your experience with customer discovery?"**

Use **JTBD application** example:
```
At Jumbo Homes, I use Jobs to Be Done framework for buyer qualification.

Instead of just matching property specs, I ask: "When did you start looking? What changed? What are you trying to achieve?"

Example: Two buyers both want a 3BR in Whitefield. But one is trying to shorten a draining commute before their kid starts school. The other wants rental income.

Same property request, completely different jobs. Understanding that changes what shortlist I show them, which properties I prioritize showing, and how I position each option.

I document this approach in an article on my portfolio—includes the decision flow, interview questions, and how to measure if you're serving the right job.
```

**"What do you want to learn next?"**

```
SQL and Mixpanel for deeper funnel analysis. Right now I can identify bottlenecks through process observation and customer conversations. I want to add quantitative diagnosis—cohort analysis, conversion metrics, retention curves.

Also prioritization frameworks (RICE, ICE) for when you have more problems than resources. I've been learning these through my CXO Learning Book project.

Not trying to become a data scientist or product manager, but these tools help me ask better questions and validate assumptions before committing resources.
```

## Salary Discussion

### When asked "What are your salary expectations?"

**Early-stage startups (< 50 people):**
```
I'm flexible on cash compensation for the right founder's office role, especially if there's meaningful equity.

My priority is owning impactful problems and learning from a strong founding team. What's the typical range for this role + equity structure?
```

**Growth-stage startups (50-200 people):**
```
For a growth operations role in Bengaluru with this scope, I'd expect ₹[8-12 LPA] base + performance bonus/equity, depending on the full package.

But I'm more focused on the role itself—can I own problems end-to-end? What's the typical comp structure for this level?
```

**Always:**
- Research typical ranges first (Glassdoor, levels.fyi, network)
- Ask about their range before stating yours
- Consider total comp: base + bonus + equity + learning opportunity
- For founder's office roles, prioritize impact > cash (if financially feasible)

## Success Metrics

### Leading Indicators (Track Weekly):
- Applications submitted
- Custom messages sent (vs. template)
- Informational chats completed
- LinkedIn profile views

### Lagging Indicators (Track Weekly):
- Screening calls scheduled
- On-sites scheduled
- Offers received

### Target Conversion Rates:
- Applications → Screening: 15-20%
- Screening → Final Round: 40-50%
- Final Round → Offer: 30-40%

**If conversion rates are lower:**
- < 10% applications → screening: Improve targeting or messaging
- < 30% screening → final: Improve interview prep or fit assessment
- < 20% final → offer: Either fit issues or negotiation/close problems

## Red Flags to Avoid

**In applications:**
- ❌ Generic "I'm passionate about your mission" language
- ❌ Applying to 50+ roles without customization
- ❌ Not researching the company before applying
- ❌ Typos in company name or role title

**In outreach:**
- ❌ Long-winded emails (keep to 150 words max)
- ❌ Asking for a job directly in first message
- ❌ Not offering value in informational chats

**In interviews:**
- ❌ Not asking about the actual problems they need solved
- ❌ Talking about what you want to learn instead of what you can contribute
- ❌ Bad-mouthing current/previous employer
