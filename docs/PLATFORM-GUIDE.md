# Platform Distribution Guide

This guide explains how to adapt your website articles for different platforms. Always publish on your website first.

---

## Publishing Priority

| Priority | Platform | Best For | Frequency |
|----------|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Core** | Your website | Complete original content, permanent portfolio | Every article |
| **Core** | LinkedIn | Professional discussion, recruiter visibility | Most articles |
| Secondary | Medium | Long-form discovery | Selected articles |
| Secondary | Substack | Opt-in readers | Monthly digest |
| Optional | X / Threads | Quick insights, discussion | Selective |
| Optional | Instagram | Visual explanations | When you have strong diagrams |
| Selective | Reddit/Quora | Answer real questions | When relevant question exists |
| Selective | GFG/LeetCode | Technical content only | Only if genuinely applicable |

**Start with your website + LinkedIn.** Add one secondary channel after you have a sustainable writing habit.

---

## General Principles

### Workflow
1. Write, review, and publish on your website
2. Verify the article is live and accessible
3. Create platform-specific adaptations
4. Review all drafts before posting
5. Post to platforms manually (no automatic cross-posting)
6. Log actual publication URLs
7. Monitor and respond to engagement

### Content Rules
- All platform drafts derive from the same website article
- Don't invent new claims or stories for a platform
- Don't contradict or substantially change your original
- Credit and link to your original article
- Track source revision so you know when drafts are stale

## LinkedIn

### LinkedIn Post (Recommended)

**Best for:** One practical insight with visual

**Format:**
- 3-5 short paragraphs
- One key takeaway or example
- Link to full article
- Optional: carousel or document attachment

**Template:**
```markdown
[Opening hook — state the problem or question]

[Your insight or framework in 2-3 sentences]

[Concrete example showing it in action]

[What this means for the reader]

Full guide: [link]
```

**Tips:**
- Lead with the insight, not "I wrote an article"
- Use short paragraphs (2-3 lines max)
- One clear takeaway before the link
- No engagement bait ("Comment below if you agree!")
- Test link before posting

**Carousel Suggestions:**
- 5-7 slides maximum
- Readable text size
- One idea per slide
- Consistent visual style
- Last slide has CTA and link

### LinkedIn Article (Optional)

**Best for:** A distinct professional angle on the topic

**Format:**
- 600-1200 words
- Different opening than website version
- Clear link to original article for full details
- Professional tone and examples

**Important:**
- A LinkedIn article is NOT a "canonical" in the technical sense
- Link to your original prominently
- Don't present it as the source — your website is
- Use this for a different angle, not just copy-paste

**When to use:**
- You want to explore one dimension more deeply
- Professional audience warrants different framing
- You have a distinct take worth a separate piece

**When to skip:**
- Most of the time — post + carousel is enough
- Article is already concise and platform-ready

## Medium

### Two Approaches

#### Approach 1: Import with Canonical (Preferred for Full Republish)
1. Use Medium's import tool: Story → Import a story
2. Paste your article URL
3. Medium attempts to set canonical automatically
4. **Verify canonical in HTML** after import
5. Check that formatting looks good
6. Publish

**Current Medium guidance:** Their import tool tries to add canonical. Verify it appears in the published HTML. 

**Limitation:** You don't control whether Medium honors the canonical fully, and Google may still choose to index Medium instead of your original. If protecting your original from duplication is critical, use Approach 2.

#### Approach 2: Adapted Excerpt (Safer for SEO)
1. Write a 400-800 word excerpt or adaptation
2. Different opening than website version
3. Link prominently to full article on your website
4. Explain: "Full article with diagrams, sources, and examples: [link]"
5. Publish as standalone Medium post

**Use Approach 2 when:**
- You want to ensure your website remains the clear source
- Article has substantial unique diagrams or interactive elements
- You're unsure about Medium's canonical handling

### Tips
- Add useful tags (4-5 relevant)
- Use Medium's built-in formatting (headers, quotes)
- Don't include all your website's diagrams if republishing
- Monitor stats to see if Medium drives qualified traffic

## Substack (Newsletter)

**Best for:** Chapter digests or monthly roundups

**Format:**
- Introduce the month's theme or articles
- 200-300 word excerpt from each article
- Link to full article on your website
- Optional: what you're working on next

**Template:**
```markdown
Subject: [Month] Chapter: [Theme]

This month I explored [theme].

## [Article 1 Title]
[2-3 paragraph excerpt highlighting key insight]
Read the full article: [link]

## [Article 2 Title]
[2-3 paragraph excerpt]
Read the full article: [link]

Next: [What you're writing about next]
```

**Important:**
- Requires Substack account setup
- Need to build subscriber list (no fake subscribers)
- Sending to subscribers requires you to authorize it
- Monthly or bi-monthly is more sustainable than weekly

**When to skip:**
- You haven't written at least 3-4 articles yet
- You don't want to maintain a newsletter
- You have no existing audience

## X (Twitter)

**Best for:** Concise insight or numbered thread

### Single Post
- One clear takeaway (280 characters)
- Link to article
- Optional: image or diagram
- Make it self-contained (useful even without clicking)

### Thread (3-7 tweets)
```markdown
1/ [Hook — the question or problem]

2/ [Your insight or framework]

3/ [Example showing it in action]

4/ [Second example or nuance]

5/ [What this means / takeaway]

6/ Full guide with interview questions and metrics: [link]
```

**Tips:**
- Each tweet should be useful standalone
- Don't force it into a thread if one tweet works
- Check current character limits for your account
- Avoid "🧵 Thread" and "Like and retweet" — let substance speak

---

## Threads

**Best for:** Conversational insight + discussion question

**Format:**
- Similar to X but slightly more casual
- Shorter than LinkedIn, more conversational than X
- Genuine discussion question (not engagement bait)
- Optional: 2-3 post sequence

**Template:**
```markdown
[Opening insight or example]

[Why this matters]

[Genuine question for the community]

Full article: [link]
```

**Tips:**
- Don't mechanically copy X thread
- Threads audience likes community discussion
- Keep it authentic and conversational

## Instagram

**Best for:** Visual explanations and diagram-focused content

**Format: Carousel (5-8 slides)**

**Slide Structure:**
1. Title slide (problem/question)
2-3. Framework or model (one idea per slide)
4-6. Example or application
7. Takeaway or next steps
8. CTA with link reference

**Caption:**
- 150-300 words
- Explain the key insight
- Call out "Link in bio" or "Link in story" (Instagram doesn't make captions clickable)
- Image alt text for each slide

**Tips:**
- Design for mobile (text must be readable on phone)
- High contrast, simple layouts
- Consistent visual style
- Export as images (JPG or PNG, 1080x1080 or 1080x1350)

**When to skip:**
- Article doesn't have strong visual components
- You don't have design resources
- Audience isn't active on Instagram

---

## Pinterest

**Best for:** Evergreen framework graphics

**Format: Vertical pin (1000x1500px)**

**Content:**
- One clear diagram or framework
- Article title
- Your name/brand
- Clean design, readable text

**Pin Description:**
- 100-200 words
- What the reader will learn
- Link to article

**When to use:**
- Article has a valuable reusable diagram
- Topic is evergreen (not time-sensitive)
- You want passive long-term discovery

---

## Reddit

**Best for:** Answering specific questions in relevant communities

**Important Rules:**
- Find a real question your article answers
- Provide a useful, self-contained answer first
- Link to article as "more detail" not as the primary value
- Disclose if required by community rules
- Follow each subreddit's self-promotion rules
- Don't spam links across multiple subreddits

**Template:**
```markdown
[Direct answer to the question in 2-3 paragraphs]

[Example if relevant]

I wrote a more detailed guide on this with [specific additional value] here: [link]

Happy to answer questions!
```

**Suitable subreddits for your content (check rules first):**
- r/entrepreneur
- r/growthmarketing (check if allowed)
- r/product_management
- r/startups

---

## Quora

**Best for:** Detailed answers to real questions

**Format:**
- 400-800 word answer
- Self-contained and useful standalone
- Link to article for "additional details"
- Professional tone

**When to use:**
- There's a real relevant question with traffic
- You can provide substantial value in the answer itself
- Link adds, doesn't replace, the value

---

## GeeksforGeeks (GFG)

**IMPORTANT:** Check current contribution policies before submission.

**Current GFG Policy (as of references checked):**
- Submissions must be original
- AI-generated content may be rejected
- Plagiarism is prohibited
- You may not control canonicals or promotional links

**When to submit:**
- Article genuinely fits GFG's technical education audience
- Content is original and not AI-generated
- Topic aligns with their categories (data structures, algorithms, interview prep, databases)

**When to skip:**
- Business frameworks (JTBD, AARRR)
- Growth strategy
- Founder's office topics
- Marketing funnels
- General product management

**For most of your articles: Create `distribution/[slug]/gfg-review.md` with status "skip — audience mismatch"**

**If submitting:**
1. Review GFG contribution guidelines
2. Ensure technical accuracy
3. Follow their formatting requirements
4. Submit via their platform
5. Track submission (not same as publication)
6. If accepted, log actual publication URL

## LeetCode

**IMPORTANT:** Only relevant for coding problems, SQL queries, or interview prep content.

**When to submit:**
- You've created SQL examples or problem-solving explanations
- Content is directly relevant to interview preparation
- Follows LeetCode community guidelines

**When to skip (most articles):**
- JTBD, funnels, growth metrics
- Business frameworks
- Product strategy
- General business writing

**For most articles: Mark as "skip — not applicable"**

## YouTube / Shorts

**Best for:** Walkthrough of a decision or funnel

**Format: Short Video (60-90 seconds)**

**Content:**
- Show one decision framework or flow
- Walk through the logic
- Call out key question or insight
- Link in description

**When to use:**
- You're comfortable on camera
- Diagram benefits from spoken explanation
- You want to demonstrate how you think

**When to skip:**
- You're not ready for video
- Article works fine as text
- Creating video is a major time investment

## WhatsApp / Telegram Groups

**Best for:** Existing communities where sharing is welcome

**Format:**
- 2-3 sentence summary
- Link to article
- Optional: one key insight or diagram

**Important:**
- Only share in groups where it's appropriate
- Don't spam across multiple groups
- Respect group rules and norms
- No unsolicited bulk distribution

## Measuring Success

### Meaningful Metrics
- Qualified profile visits (recruiters, founders, operators)
- Thoughtful comments or questions
- Conversations started (DMs, emails)
- Interview requests
- Résumé downloads from relevant people

### Vanity Metrics (Don't Optimize For These Alone)
- Total impressions
- Likes without engagement
- Followers who never interact
- Page views without context

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Posting everywhere at once** — Focus on 1-2 platforms well
2. **Copying the exact same text** — Adapt for each platform's audience and format
3. **Leading with "I wrote an article"** — Lead with the insight
4. **Not linking to your original** — Always credit your website
5. **Posting before website is live** — Website first, always
6. **Inventing new claims** — Stay consistent with your original
7. **Ignoring platform rules** — Read and follow guidelines
8. **Measuring only vanity metrics** — Track qualified interest
9. **Forcing content onto irrelevant platforms** — It's okay to skip
10. **Not logging what you published** — Track URLs and learn what works
