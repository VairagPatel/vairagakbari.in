# A publishing system for Vairag

Publish the full article on vairagakbari.in first. Then adapt the idea to a few channels. The recommendations below are editorial choices for a founder’s office, strategy, growth, and product audience; they are not promises of reach or search ranking.

| Priority | Platform | What to publish | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core | Your website | Complete article, original diagram, sources, worked example | Permanent portfolio and searchable archive |
| Core | LinkedIn | A concise lesson with a document carousel; occasional article/newsletter | Recruiters, founders, operators and professional discussion |
| Secondary | Medium | An adapted or republished article, with canonical pointing to your original | Additional long-form discovery |
| Secondary | Substack | A monthly chapter digest with excerpts and links | An opt-in readership for the book |
| Optional | X / Threads | One argument or short thread with the visual | Discussion and testing hooks |
| Optional | Instagram | A short carousel: problem, flowchart, example, takeaway | Visual explanations |
| Optional | Pinterest | A readable vertical framework graphic linked to the article | Visual discovery |
| Optional | YouTube / Shorts | A short walkthrough of one funnel or business decision | Demonstrate how you explain and reason |
| Selective | Reddit / Quora | A self-contained useful answer to an existing question | Feedback and relevant conversations; follow each community’s rules |
| Selective | Product, growth and founder communities | A relevant example or diagram when community rules allow it | Specialist feedback and relationships |
| Selective | WhatsApp / Telegram groups | A short summary for groups that welcome it | Existing community distribution |

Start with your website and LinkedIn. Add one secondary channel after a sustainable writing habit exists. You do not need to be active everywhere.

## One article → one week of useful distribution
1. Write one question, one visual model, one worked example, and one practical takeaway. Identify assumptions and cite sources.
2. Publish the complete article on your site. Add it to the article index and the appropriate book chapter.
3. Share a LinkedIn post with the key insight and a short carousel. Link to the original for the full explanation.
4. Reuse the diagram in ONE additional channel; adapt the caption to that audience.
5. Answer comments. Use objections and questions to improve the article.
6. Once a month, send an opt-in chapter digest if you have established a newsletter. No newsletter service is connected to this project.

## SEO and measurement
- Use unique page titles and descriptions, readable headings, original examples, source links, and related-article links.
- /article/Job-To-Be-Done/#sub-topic is a section link. The canonical page is /article/Job-To-Be-Done/ without the fragment. Give a subtopic its own page only when it merits a complete article.
- Medium’s import tool automatically adds a canonical to the source. Verify it after importing. Canonicals are signals; they do not guarantee Google’s choice.
- For newsletters and platforms without a suitable canonical workflow, publish a useful excerpt or a distinct adaptation linking to the full article.
- Social distribution can bring readers and conversations; posting on many sites does not automatically increase rankings.
- Optional tracking example: ?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=jtbd. Keep the canonical clean. No analytics tracking is installed in this website.
- Review qualified profile visits, article readers, résumé interest, conversations, and interview leads. Do not optimise only for impressions.

## Starter editorial sequence (proposals, not completed work)
1. Jobs to Be Done — included as the first draft.
2. A revenue tree: users × purchase frequency × average order value; adapt definitions to each business model.
3. AARRR: where the funnel loses people and what evidence you need next.
4. Retention cohorts: what repeat behaviour can tell you.
5. A prioritisation decision using RICE or ICE, with assumptions visible.
6. GTM agents: automate preparation, keep judgment and approvals with people.
